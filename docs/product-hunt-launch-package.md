**Title:** The math behind "CTC" – How a ₹25 LPA offer can leave you with a surprisingly low take-home salary

Hi everyone,

I wanted to share a detailed breakdown of how compensation packages are structured in India right now, and why the standard "LPA" headline figure recruiters throw at you is often highly misleading.

I’m the builder of CareerOS, an Indian-focused career platform. I built it because I’m tired of seeing junior and mid-level devs accept offers that look amazing on LinkedIn, but barely cover high rent in HSR Layout or Gurgaon once taxes and deductions hit.

Let's look at a typical **₹25 LPA CTC** offer for a Software Engineer:

1. **One-time Sign-on Bonus (e.g., ₹2 Lakhs)**: Recruiters add this to the headline CTC to inflate it. In Year 2, your package instantly drops by ₹2L.
2. **Performance/Variable Pay (e.g., ₹3 Lakhs)**: This is "discretionary." In a bad funding environment, many startups pay out 0% of this, yet they count 100% of it in your CTC.
3. **Employer PF & Gratuity (e.g., ₹1.2 Lakhs)**: This is technically part of the cost to the company, but it’s not cash in your hand today.
4. **ESOPs/RSUs (e.g., ₹5 Lakhs)**: Vesting is typically 25% per year over 4 years. That means only ₹1.25L is real each year, and only *if* the company exits or provides a buyback.

Your actual cash base is **₹13.8 Lakhs**. 

If you choose the New Tax Regime, your monthly take-home salary is roughly **₹96,000**. On a "₹25 Lakhs" offer, you are taking home under ₹1 Lakh a month. 

I’ve built a free tool to calculate this instantly, including old vs. new tax slabs and gratuity caps. You can check it out without signing up: **https://career-os.in/ctc-decoder**

If you're negotiating an offer right now, make sure you negotiate the *base salary*, not the CTC. Hope this helps someone avoid a bad deal!

Hi everyone,

With the ongoing hiring season and high tech switches across metropolitan hubs, I want to discuss a major issue in the Indian job market: the lack of salary transparency and the misleading nature of "CTC" (Cost to Company).

In India, compensation is famously opaque. While companies in the US and Europe are slowly being forced to publish salary ranges by law, Indian job seekers are still asked: *"What is your current CTC?"* and *"What are your expectations?"* before any budget is shared.

Compounding this is the practice of inflating CTC packages. Joining bonuses, artificial variable structures, gratuity, employer insurance contributions, and paper-only ESOP stock allocations are lumped together to make a ₹20 Lakhs offer look massive, even when the monthly cash take-home is barely enough to keep up with high inflation in Bangalore, Mumbai, or Delhi.

I believe the only way to combat this is through open, crowd-sourced data and radical transparency. 

To help job seekers, I built a free, open utility called the **CareerOS CTC Decoder** (**https://career-os.in/ctc-decoder**).
